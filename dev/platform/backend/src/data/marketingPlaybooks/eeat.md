## E‑E‑A‑T + CITE content quality

Grade content the way Google's Quality Rater Guidelines do — on **Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust** — and, separately, on how **citable** it is to AI answer engines (CITE). Trust is the single most heavily weighted factor; a page can't outrank its trust ceiling.

**Experience** — first-hand signals: tested / measured / visited / built it, original photography (not stock), and specific detail only a practitioner would include. Generic explainer prose with no lived detail scores low here however polished.

**Expertise** — a named author with relevant standing (`knowsAbout`), correct and current subject treatment, and depth proportional to the query's difficulty. YMYL topics (health, finance, safety, and — per Sept 2025 QRG — major political/social topics) are held to a higher bar.

**Authoritativeness** — cite authoritative external sources rather than asserting; corroborate claims; be a recognised entity on the topic. Outbound citations to primary sources raise this; a closed page that references nothing lowers it.

**Trust (weighted heaviest)** — HTTPS, a reachable contact/about, clear authorship, visible publish/updated dates, honest and non-deceptive framing, and transparency about sources and limitations. Missing authorship or dates, or unverifiable claims, cap the whole page.

**CITE (AI citation-readiness)** — write self-contained answer passages of ~130–170 words an engine can lift verbatim; use question-shaped headings that match real queries; attribute statistics to sources; keep a healthy citation-to-claim ratio. Evidence-based caveats: llms.txt is not a citation lever, arbitrary content-chunking isn't required, and AI-specific keyword rewriting is unnecessary — quotable, well-sourced passages are what get cited.

**When auditing:** grade each factor A–F against the evidence on the page, name the specific gap (not "improve trust" but "no author byline and no publish date — add both"), and give a publish verdict: **publish** (strong), **revise** (fixable gaps), or **rework** (below the bar). **When drafting:** write to this standard from the start — real author, sources, dates, first-hand detail, and liftable answer passages.
