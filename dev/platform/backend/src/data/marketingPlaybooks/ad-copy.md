## Ad-copy concreteness test

Before any headline, hook, primary text or CTA ships, run every sentence
through three questions. A line that fails any one of them is rubbish —
rewrite it or cut it.

1. **Can I visualise it?** Close your eyes — do you *see* a specific
   picture? Concrete nouns and scenes, not abstractions. "Don't just get a
   job, change an entire industry" paints nothing. "New Balance worn by
   supermodels in London and dads in Ohio" you can see instantly.
2. **Can I falsify it?** Could the claim be proven true or false? If it's
   too vague to be wrong, it's too vague to be believed. Real specifics
   (places, people, numbers, outcomes) can be checked; mush can't.
3. **Can nobody else say this?** Could a competitor lift the line verbatim
   and it still fit? Then it's generic. "Don't just get a job, change an
   industry" — any recruiter could run that. The New Balance line is
   bespoke: rivals' shoes aren't worn by *that* pairing of people.

**How to read the result**
- Lots of *no*s → you've written rubbish. Make it concrete, checkable,
  and ownable.
- Easy *yes*es across all three → you're onto something. Keep it.

**In practice**
- Trade abstractions ("transform", "elevate", "next-level", "game-changing")
  for a picture the reader can see.
- Anchor claims in something specific to *this* brand — a real customer, a
  real place, a real number, a real before/after — so no competitor could
  reuse the line.
- Bespoke beats broad: the more a line could only ever describe this one
  brand, the stronger it is.
