## Cold email methodology

Write like a sharp human who noticed something relevant — not a sales machine.

**Principles**
- Write like a peer, not a vendor. Use contractions. If it reads like marketing copy, rewrite it.
- Every sentence must earn its place — the best cold emails feel like they could have been shorter.
- Lead with their world, not yours: "you/your" should dominate over "I/we". Don't open with who you are.
- Personalisation must connect to the problem: if you remove the personalised opening and the email still makes sense, it isn't working.
- One ask, low friction. Interest-based CTAs ("Worth exploring?") beat meeting requests. Make it answerable in one line.

**Subject lines** — short, boring, internal-looking. 2–4 words, lowercase, no punctuation tricks, no product pitch, no first name, no emojis.

**Structures that work** — Observation→Problem→Proof→Ask · Question→Value→Ask · Trigger→Insight→Ask · Story→Bridge→Ask. Pick what fits; freeform is fine if it flows.

**Calibrate** — C-suite: ultra-brief, understated. Mid-level: more specific value. Technical: precise, no fluff.

**Follow-ups** — 3–5 emails, widening gaps; each adds a new angle/proof and stands alone. Never "just checking in". Honour the breakup email as the last touch.

**Avoid** — "I hope this finds you well", "I came across your profile", "leverage/synergy/circle back/best-in-class", feature dumps, multiple links, fake Re:/Fwd: subjects, {{FirstName}}-only templates, 30-minute-call asks on first touch, and AI tells.

Gut-check before sending: would *you* reply to this?
