# The Desk

1-bit Dither · 1920×1080 (960×540 internal) · 30 fps · ~55 s · no narration, all text in the world

Source: *Industry Resources 2027*, Atlanta Design Festival. Audience: a brand deciding whether to
take a quarter. The brief was: lead with the hook, go faster, pack it with information.

The hook, as the client wrote it: **get your brand in front of buyers for one quarter of the year,
in Atlanta and the Southeast.** That line is the first thing on screen and the last thing the film
pulls back to.

---

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A · A relay of media.** Screen → signal → print → page, the style's own suggested chain. True to
the medium, but the chain is about transmission and this film is about what a sponsor owns.

**B · One instant explored by camera.** A single frozen desk, the camera finding each artefact.
Beautiful, and far too slow for a brief that asks for pace.

**C · A desk assembling a quarter, cut on the beat.** ← chosen
One desk under one hard lamp. Every item a sponsor buys arrives on it as a physical, printed thing:
the list on a screen, the edition coming out of a printer line by line, three banner cards, three
social cards, the guide, the calendar, the price stamped. Hard cuts on the beat, macro locks for
reading, an inversion strike on every figure that matters.

Chosen because the kit's content is a list of deliverables, and in this medium a deliverable can be
a real object with its text printed on it. It also gives the pace the brief asked for: a cut every
1.5 to 2 seconds, twenty-two pieces of information in fifty-five seconds, with no narration to wait
for.

## 2. Logline and arc

> A desk under a lamp assembles one quarter of a design festival's owned media, piece by printed
> piece, and then pulls back far enough to show whose desks it reaches.

**Hook** (0–7 s) An inversion strike, and the offer stamped on paper.
**The audience** (7–17 s) The list on a screen, printing a row at a time. 8,911, then the three rates.
**What a quarter is** (17–33 s) Four artefacts, hard cuts: the edition, the banners, the posts, the guide.
**When and how much** (33–47 s) The calendar, four slots, one sponsor each. The price, struck.
**The reach** (47–55 s) Fall into the guide's dot, pull back to Atlanta and the Southeast.

## 3. Benchmark

**Learn from:** the discipline of ordered-dither first-person 1-bit rendering, where tone is density
and the pattern is nailed to the surface; wire-photo prints for how text survives a coarse screen.

**Do not take:** any named game's characters, scenes, fonts or palettes, and no naming of them in
the film or the credits.

## 4. The two inks

| | |
|---|---|
| Ink | `#15110F` |
| Paper | `#EFE3D2` |

No third colour, including on the end card. Value does the work: the lamp is hard, the shadows are
solid ink, and every frame keeps one large pure-ink mass (the dark of the room beyond the lamp) and
one open paper mass (the lit card being read).

## 5. Shot list

Cuts land on the beat at 120 BPM. Every text card is held long enough to read, and the camera
drifts rather than cutting while a line is being read.

| # | Shot | Camera | Dur | Why |

| 02 | A screen on the desk, rows printing one at a time | Macro, slow drift | 5 | The audience, arriving as a list rather than a claim |
| 03 | `8,911` stamped on the card beside it | Locked, inversion on landing | 3 | The number, struck |
| 04 | Three rate cards: 39%, 19%, 0.2% | Hard cuts, one per beat | 4 | Three figures in four seconds |

| 07 | Three banner cards laid down, sizes stencilled | Hard cuts | 4 | 728×90, 300×250, 160×600 |

## 7. Cue map

A small, cold ensemble under mechanical sound, as the style asks. 120 BPM.

| Section | Machine | Ensemble |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | relay click, stamp on cork | one bass clarinet note, long |
| The audience | print head stepping, two per beat | pizzicato low strings on the beat |
| The rates | three stamps | glockenspiel, one note per card |
| The edition | printer motor as the pulse, scan tick as hi-hat | detuned upright piano, four-note figure |
| The banners | card slaps | pizzicato continues, bass clarinet underneath |
| The guide | paper settling | piano alone, slower |
| The calendar | a page turning, a drawer | clarinet and glockenspiel |
| **The price** | **one stamp, then nothing** | **drop everything for two beats** |
| The pull-back | motor fading | bass clarinet and glockenspiel, resolving |

The one real silence is the price: the style says to drop all music for a verdict hold and keep a
single far, filtered element so the silence is not dead. Here that element is the printer motor,
two rooms away.

## 8. Sound design

Mechanical and printed throughout: relays, a print head stepping, a drum motor, stamps on cork,
card slaps, a page turning, a drawer, switches. No electronics, no whoosh, nothing synthetic-sounding.
Little energy below 45 Hz, as the medium's mix note asks.

## 9. Type in the world

There are no overlay captions: every word is printed, typed or stamped on an object and dithered
with it. Roles, as the style sets them:

- **Stamps and stencils**: a heavy sans, large, biased to ink so it survives the 960×540 grid.
- **Labels and the screen**: a typewriter face, stroked.
- **The printed edition and the guide**: a condensed grotesk for headings, with body text as
  printed lines rather than readable copy, because a reader is meant to recognise a page, not read it.

Latin cap height stays at or above 9 internal pixels for anything that must be read, which is 18 px
at 1080p, and every text card sits on its own pale surface rather than on a mid-tone one.

## 10. What this film is not

It is not the kit, and it is not the spy title sequence done again in another palette. That one was
a chase with a courier and a package; this one is a desk, a lamp and a stack of printed evidence,
and it carries roughly three times as many figures in four fifths of the running time.

No narration. The brief asked for density and pace, and in this medium text on an object is faster
than a voice: a stamp lands in two frames and is read in one second, where a spoken line of the
same content costs four.
