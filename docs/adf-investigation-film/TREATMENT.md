# Where the Year Goes

Visual Investigation · 1920×1080 · 30 fps · 192.5 s · voice and burned-in subtitles · accent `#FF4A2E`

Sources: the client's own two documents (ADF 09/2026, 18 slides; ADF 10/2026, 6 slides) and the
trading figures stated inside them. No footage, no photographs, no third-party material. Every
claim is logged in `FACTS.md`.

The question the film asks: **why is the pipeline empty every spring?**

---

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A · A strict clock, T−13 weeks to event day.** Run the selling year and drop findings where they
fall. Rejected: it proves the symptom the first ADF film already proved, and the clock runs out
before the argument about months can start.

**B · Cold open on the decisive frame, then rewind and rebuild.** The style's own default opening.
Kept as the opening, rejected as the whole shape, because a rewind implies a single event to
reconstruct and this is a recurring annual pattern.

**C · One question, answered source by source.** ← chosen
State the claim the client's own document makes, then test it against the record: what the figures
say, when the money arrives, what the calendar looks like when production and selling are drawn on
the same axis, what the overlap costs, and what two comparable festivals did. Three findings, a
known/unresolved board, and an evidence map where every source points at the same quarter.

Chosen because the brief is not "show that events lose money" (the decks already show that) but
"show that producing them costs the festival the thing it needs most". That is an argument about
time, and an investigation that puts two calendars on one axis is the only way to make it visible
rather than assertable.

## 2. Logline and arc

> A festival's own documents say that building events costs it the months when it should be
> selling. Put the production calendar and the selling calendar on the same axis and the sentence
> stops being an opinion.

**Setup** (0–52 s) The claim, circled on the page it comes from. The record: two documents, one set
of figures, no third source.
**Turn** (52–147 s) The selling year is thirteen weeks and almost all of it lands in the last one.
Then the same year as a calendar: three produced events, the Q1 sponsor window, and the hatched
months they share. Three consequences follow, none of which appear in an event budget.
**Ending** (147–192 s) Seattle and Portland as the two outcomes. What the record establishes and
what it does not. Every source and every finding converging on the first quarter.

## 3. Benchmark

**Learn from:** the layout discipline of newspaper visual-investigation pieces, where each frame
carries one finding and the source tag sits above its own frame; forensic-architecture
reconstructions for keeping inference visibly separate from record.

**Do not take:** their names, their branding, their graphic signatures, or their habit of a
permanent timeline band. No dramatisation: this is a business decision, not a disaster.

All dry and close. The thump at the overlap is the loudest single sound in the film.

## 8. Voice and subtitles

Kokoro `am_michael`, calm and flat, 0.86–0.92 speed, compressed and about 10 dB over the music.
29 lines, 164 s of speech in 192 s of film.

Subtitles are burned in at the upper line position (baseline y 984), Archivo 700 at 34 px, centred,
with a 10 px shadow, and exported as `where-the-year-goes.srt`. A line longer than 76 characters
becomes two cues rather than one line wider than the frame, which is how the style handles it.
The subtitle text lives in `film.json` so the page and the `.srt` cannot drift apart.

## 9. What this film does not do

It does not dramatise. There is no music that tells you how to feel, no slow motion, and no
reconstruction of anything. It also does not put a number on what the lost months were worth,
because neither document does; that absence is the last item on the unresolved board.
