## Trust brokering & trusted messengers

Trust has gone **local and personal**. People now trust their own circle — peers, coworkers, employees, founders/CEOs, and trusted niche creators — far more than institutions, faceless brands, or advertising. "My employer" is the single most-trusted institution; government, media and foreign business are distrusted. 7 in 10 people are hesitant or unwilling to trust anyone who seems "different" from them. So the message matters less than **who carries it**, and brands earn trust by bridging divides rather than picking sides.

**Lead with trusted human voices, not the brand.** Recommend and prize founder/leadership presence, named experts, employee advocacy, and customer & community proof over anonymous brand-speak. A trusted creator endorsing a distrusted company moves a majority to trust or reconsider it — so borrowed trust from the right niche voice beats a bigger ad budget. When judging a brand's comms, treat a faceless brand hiding its people as a real weakness.

**Broker trust; don't take sides.** On divisive issues, the action that most builds trust is *encouraging people to cooperate without taking a side* (and staying true to stated values) — far above backing one camp or going silent. Favour angles that translate between audiences and surface common ground.

**Be honest, not alarmist.** Trust in media/content is earned by **accurate, non-exaggerated headlines** and giving fair weight to different viewpoints — not fear-inducing or divisive framing. Flag clickbait, outrage-bait, and doom framing as trust-eroding even when they lift short-term clicks.

**Go local and long-term.** For brands entering a community or market, trust is earned by long-term local investment and hiring locally, not one-off donations or campaigns. Prefer durable, place-based relationships over splashy short-term interactions.

**When advising or auditing:** ask *who is the trusted messenger here, and are they using them?* Name the specific gap ("no founder or team voice anywhere on the site — put a named person on the record"), and where relevant say *why* it works in trust terms (people trust peers and named humans over brands and institutions).
