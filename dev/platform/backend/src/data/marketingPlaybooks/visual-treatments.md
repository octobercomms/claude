## Modern visual treatments

When writing the *visual concept* / art direction for a creative, reach
for current, premium aesthetic treatments instead of generic stock-photo
descriptions — but only where they fit the brand. The look should serve
the message, not decorate it.

A working vocabulary to draw on:
- **Gradient mesh / aurora gradients** — rich, multi-stop colour washes
  and soft blurred blends for backgrounds; modern and depth-y without
  imagery.
- **Liquid metal / chrome** — reflective, fluid metallic surfaces for
  logos, type or hero objects; high-end, trend-forward.
- **Glassmorphism** ("liquid glass") — frosted, translucent panels,
  cards and buttons with blur and subtle borders; clean and contemporary.
- **3D render / dimensional scenes** — soft-lit 3D objects or environments
  for a polished, expensive feel.
- Supporting touches: subtle grain/noise, duotone, bold editorial type,
  generous negative space.

**How to apply**
- Name a specific treatment in the visual concept where it suits the
  brand's positioning (premium / tech / design-led brands especially) —
  e.g. "frosted glass card over an aurora-gradient background" beats
  "clean modern background".
- Match the treatment to the brand kit (palette, type, logo), don't
  override it. A heritage or trades brand may want none of this — restraint
  is a valid choice.
- Pick one or two treatments per concept; stacking all of them reads as
  noise. The effect should make the offer clearer or more desirable, not
  just trendier.
