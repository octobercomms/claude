# A Festival Convenes

Swiss Motion Graphics · 1920×1080 · 24 fps · 108 s · no voice, no subtitles · signal colour `#D8432A`

Source: *A festival convenes. It does not produce.* Atlanta Design Festival, the twentieth year.
Prepared for Bernard by Daniel, October 2026. Six slides. Audience: Bernard. Every figure, name and
quoted phrase on screen is from that deck.

---

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A · The calendar.** Fifty-two weeks, two of which earn, and the year slowly fills with work that
returns nothing. Honest, and it is the deck's sharpest fact. Rejected as the spine because it is one
image, not an argument: it proves the problem and says nothing about what to do.

**B · The five decisions as a countdown.** Open on November and work backwards. Rejected because the
first film made for this client already ends on a deadline, and because a countdown makes the ask feel
like pressure rather than arithmetic.

**C · The subtraction.** ← chosen
The film performs the deck's own verb. A word is cut out of a sentence; three produced events are cut
out of the year; instruments are cut out of the band as each one goes. What is left re-flows into two
things that need no production and five decisions with dates on them. The sentence returns at the end
with the word still missing.

Chosen because the deck's argument is a removal, and this style's native move for removal is the knife
cut: a line crosses an element and it collapses to its midline. Making the cut carry the argument means
the medium and the message are the same gesture. It also separates this film from the first one made
for ADF, which was an additive structure: one grid that kept being re-read.

## 2. Logline and arc

> A festival discovers that the thing it is proudest of building is the thing costing it the year, and
> watches itself get smaller in exactly the right places.

**Setup** (0–27 s) The sentence loses a word. Four figures say the year is going backwards, and an ink
bar names the cause as production rather than effort.
**Turn** (27–74 s) Fifty-two weeks, two of which earn. Five assets, none of which need an event. Then
the three produced events are cut away one at a time, and the band thins with them until only the bass
is left.
**Ending** (74–108 s) A nonprofit and a price list. Five decisions with dates. Everything is cut away,
and the sentence comes back without its word.

## 3. Benchmark

**Learn from:** the editing grammar of Saul Bass's *Walk on the Wild Side* titles for marks that move
with intent rather than decoration; Swiss railway timetable sheets for how a dense list stays readable;
the first ADF film's own restraint, so the two sit together as a pair.

**Do not take:** any historical poster composition, Helvetica or Akzidenz, any institution's mark. The
six institutions appear as set type in a list, never as logos.

## 8. Title and caption design

No subtitles: there is no speech. Archivo throughout, one family.

- **The sentence** 104 px, weight 600, −2 % tracking, optically flush left on a 3 px rule.
- **Giant figures** 108 px, weight 700, fitted to their module.
- **Statements** 54 px, weight 600, flush left at the left margin.
- **Caption rows** 19 px, weight 500, with a 1 px rule above.
- **Date chips** 20 px, weight 600, reversed out of the signal colour, except TWO WEEKS which is ink,
  because it is the only one that is not a month.

## 9. One thing the film deliberately does not say

The deck states that two of the six revenue lines have ever been sold, and does not say which two. The
film shows six marks with two filled rather than striking through four named lines, so it carries the
claim exactly as far as the deck does.
