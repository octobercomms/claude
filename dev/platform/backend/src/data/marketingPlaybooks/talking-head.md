## Talking-to-camera delivery

Applies whenever a frame is filmed to camera — the Video Style System
**style B (talking head anchor)**, and any piece-to-camera voiceover the
host delivers on screen. Good words fail if the delivery doesn't connect,
so direct the *performance*, not just the script.

Three things separate "reading an autocue" from "talking to a person":

1. **Make eye contact with the lens** as if you're talking to one person
   standing right behind it — not at yourself, not at the screen.
2. **Look away sometimes**, the way you naturally would mid-conversation —
   constant unbroken staring reads as intense or robotic. Glance away on a
   thinking beat, then come back to the lens.
3. **Pause at the end of a sentence and hold the eye contact for one
   second** before moving on. That held beat is what lands the point and
   makes the viewer feel spoken *to*.

**How to apply when writing talking-head frames**
- For each style-B frame, give a short *delivery note* alongside the
  voiceover line — where to hold the lens, where to glance away, where to
  land a one-second pause (e.g. after the hook and before the CTA).
- Keep sentences short enough to deliver in one breath with a clean pause
  at the end — long run-on lines kill the held-eye-contact beat.
- Open on the lens (hook straight down the barrel), allow a natural look-
  away in the middle, and return to the lens to close.
