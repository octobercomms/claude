# The Quarter

60s Spy Title Sequence · 1920×1080 · 24 fps · ~70 s · tape-briefing voice, subtitles on a paper strip

Source: *Industry Resources 2027*, Atlanta Design Festival, 24-page sales kit. Audience: a brand
considering a quarter. The film's job is to make a sponsor want the slot and understand what it is
in about a minute; the kit does the specification.

---

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A · A countdown.** Four quarters as four locations, a clock built from type in each, the slot
counting down to sold. Rejected: a countdown makes scarcity the whole argument, and scarcity from a
festival nobody has sponsored yet reads as pressure rather than value.

**B · Two agents, one frame.** The brand on one side of a diagonal, the specifier on the other,
meeting in the middle. True to how the product works, but it needs two rigs and the meeting is the
only beat; the middle of the film has nothing to do.

**C · A courier on a deadline.** ← chosen
One silhouette carries one package through the four pieces a sponsor buys: the list, the inbox, the
network, the guide. The package is the brand's message, the brightest thing in frame on a black
backing. It is set down at the end and becomes the title.

Chosen because the product literally is a delivery: ADF carries one sponsor's message to 8,911
specifiers through four channels over three months. A courier structure means every location is a
thing the sponsor is buying, the figure's progress is the campaign's progress, and the style's own
native move — the object of the chase becomes the title — lands on the product name without a
single sales line.

## 2. Logline and arc

> One courier, one package, four rooms built out of the festival's own numbers, and a price that
> stops the music.

**Setup** (0–10 s) A hand sets a package on a cream ground. One stab. The assignment.
**The run** (10–48 s) Four locations, each a credit line that is also its architecture: a column of
names, a letter O that becomes the inbox, three buildings along a lateral track, a page that stays
standing after the quarter closes.
**The turn** (48–58 s) Stop-time on the price. Everything freezes in silence. $2,500.
**Ending** (58–70 s) The package is set down and the pieces fly back along the diagonal to assemble
the title. Four slots, one sponsor each.

## 3. Benchmark

**Learn from:** Saul Bass's *Anatomy of a Murder* for type as structure and the confidence of flat
cut shapes; Kuntzel + Deygas's *Catch Me If You Can* for a lateral cut-paper chase that stays
graphic rather than illustrative.

**Do not take:** any real title's lettering, logo, characters or shots. No gun barrel, no panther,
no chromatic spy-theme crawl, no minor-major-ninth ending chord. The score is an original motif.

## 4. The four inks

| Ink | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ink black | `#17130F` | the courier, type, keylines |
| Paper cream | `#EFE3CC` | the dominant ground, and the paper the film is cut from |
| Hot | `#D8432A` | the festival's own red: the courier's scarf, and the package |
| Mustard | `#E0A32B` | the secondary: the object the courier is carrying toward |

One dominant ground per location, changing on the cut: cream, then black, then mustard, then cream.
Shadow-paper variants of each for depth. Nothing else.

## 7. Cue map

Original motif in D minor, 132 BPM, 1960s spy big band, all synthesised.

| Section | Instrumentation |
|---|---|
| The assignment | Walking upright bass, brushes, one vibraphone figure |
| The list | Add low surf-guitar twang through spring reverb, bongos |
| The inbox | Flute and vibraphone over the bass; brass out |
| The network | Full stacked brass, chromatic dotted figure, shaker |
| The guide | Brass thins to organ stabs on the off-beats |
| **Stop-time** | **Nothing. True zero, reverb tails cut** |
| The hand-off | Band returns on the downbeat with a crash |
| The title | The big chord, then a short button |

Stabs at bars 2, 6, 13, 20, 29 and 37 are the edit points, placed 8 ms early so the peak lands on
the frame. Two silences: the stop-time at bar 29, and a one-beat gap before the title chord.

| The list | footsteps on stairs in tempo, paper slides as letters pass |
| The inbox | a tape click, a blind flip, one envelope tear |

| The guide | paper settling, a pen tick per arrow |

All paper and tape. No gadgets, no electronics, no gunshots.

## 9. Voice

A briefing on tape: band-passed 220–5200 Hz, soft saturation, slow wow, hiss rising underneath.
Six very short lines, as the style asks, never over a stab:

1. "Eight thousand nine hundred and eleven people who specify, buy and recommend."
2. "One edition. Your subject. Nobody else in it."
3. "Three sites. A full quarter of display."
4. "A guide that keeps working after the quarter closes."
5. "Two thousand five hundred dollars. Four slots in the year."
6. "One sponsor. One quarter."

Subtitles on a narrow scissor-cut paper strip, bottom-left, League Spartan 600 at 44 px, light strip
on the dark scenes and dark on the light ones, sliding in from the left in 0.18 s.

## 10. What this film is not

It is not the kit. It carries six numbers and one price, and sends a brand to the page for the rest.
It does not promise reach it cannot evidence: every figure on screen is from the kit, and the
performance line is the kit's own ("around 3,500 opens and 1,700 clicks on past performance"),
which is why the film quotes rates rather than guarantees.
